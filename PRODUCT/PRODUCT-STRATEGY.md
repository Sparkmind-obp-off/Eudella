# EUDELLA — Product Strategy

## Product thesis

People should be able to access practical support without having to coordinate every source, tool, and next step alone. EUDELLA aims to provide a coherent support layer that starts from a person's own stated needs and helps them move from question to informed action.

This is a product thesis to validate, not a proven market outcome.

## Product lines

### EUDELLA Personal Life Support (B2C)
A private personal workspace where a user can:
- describe a need or goal in their own words;
- explore relevant life areas without being forced into categories;
- discover information, services, and opportunities;
- compare options and understand trade-offs;
- create and revise plans;
- manage authorized tasks and integrations;
- inspect and control personal data.

### EUDELLA Organizational Support (B2B)
A later product line for organizations to publish, organize, and route support resources. Individual-level support conversations and private context must remain private by default. Organization administrators may see only appropriately aggregated or explicitly shared information.

## Initial MVP scope

1. Private sign-in and user-controlled profile.
2. Consent and permission center.
3. Ask for Support intake.
4. Resource and information directory with source metadata.
5. Support plan builder.
6. Task and reminder list (user initiated).
7. Review-and-approve action queue.
8. Data export and deletion request.
9. Audit history visible to the user.

## Out of scope for initial MVP

- Autonomous payments or financial decisions.
- Diagnosis, treatment, or clinical triage.
- Emergency response guarantees.
- Unapproved third-party contact.
- Hidden monitoring or background inference of sensitive needs.
- Employee scoring, productivity surveillance, or manager access to personal histories.
- Claims of universal or fully autonomous life management.

## User journey

1. **Arrive:** Understand what EUDELLA does and does not do.
2. **Choose:** Select a support area or describe a need freely.
3. **Consent:** Decide what information may be used and for what purpose.
4. **Explore:** Receive options, sources, and limitations.
5. **Plan:** Choose a path and edit the steps.
6. **Approve:** Review any action that affects external systems or people.
7. **Reflect:** Mark what helped, what changed, and what should be forgotten.

## Core modules (progressive rollout)

- Life Navigator
- Information & Resource Discovery
- Planning & Goals
- Education & Learning
- Career & Opportunities
- Daily Life Coordination
- Financial Organization (non-advisory at first)
- Wellbeing Resources (non-clinical)
- Relationships & Community Resources
- Safety Information & Help Escalation

Modules should be optional. The product must not assume that every module is relevant to every user.

## Success metrics

Measure usefulness and user control, not dependency:
- User-reported task usefulness.
- Time to locate a relevant resource.
- Percentage of plans users voluntarily mark as helpful.
- Completion of user-selected next steps.
- Permission comprehension.
- Trust and perceived control.
- Data export/deletion request completion.
- Safety incidents and incorrect high-confidence recommendations.

Avoid maximizing session time, compulsive return, emotional dependence, or number of personal details collected.

## Pilot design

- Small, invitation-based, voluntary pilot.
- Use synthetic data until privacy and security controls are implemented.
- Collect only feedback necessary for product validation.
- Obtain explicit consent for any personal-data processing.
- Provide a clear exit and deletion path.
- No real personal onboarding until security, privacy, and support boundaries are reviewed.

## Commercial validation

Test whether people value:
- continuity across support tasks;
- reliable resource discovery;
- practical planning and follow-through;
- trusted integration of services.

Do not set subscription pricing until interviews and pilot evidence support it.
